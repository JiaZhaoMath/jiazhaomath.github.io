  • X. Zhang, J. Zhao, D. Yang and M. Jiang*A new class of decoupled and energy-stable numerical schemes for the binary phase field crystal modelJournal of Scientific Computing, 2026, in press.DOI
    Abstract

    In this paper, we introduce a new class of energy-stable, fully decoupled numerical schemes for the binary phase-field crystal (BPFC) model, which is governed by a pair of coupled sixth-order Cahn--Hilliard-type PDEs. The key idea is to split the mobility operator using an operator splitting method. Our method decouples the two phase variables at each time step, significantly reducing computational complexity while preserving unconditional energy stability. We rigorously establish energy dissipation for both first- and second-order accurate schemes in time. Numerical convergence tests confirm the expected temporal accuracy. A series of numerical examples on regular domains, irregular geometries, surfaces, and 3D domains demonstrates the robustness and flexibility of the proposed schemes in capturing complex phase transitions in binary colloidal crystals. The results demonstrate the applicability of the proposed schemes to the BPFC model for simulating multi-component crystalline structures across a range of model parameters and domain geometries.

  • X. Jing and J. Zhao*Relaxed Lagrange multiplier (RLM) schemes for phase field models preserving the relaxed original energy dissipation lawJournal of Computational Physics, 567, 115316, 2026.DOI
    Abstract

    Phase-field models are typically derived from variational principles for a free-energy functional and are widely used to simulate complex multiphase phenomena in science and engineering. A central goal in designing numerical schemes for these models is to preserve the underlying energy-dissipation law. In this paper, we propose a class of relaxed Lagrange multiplier (RLM) schemes for phase field models. In contrast to popular scalar auxiliary variable (SAV) and invariant energy quadratization (IEQ) methods, which dissipate a modified energy involving auxiliary variables, the RLM schemes dissipate a relaxed version of the original energy and closely track the original energy dissipation rate. Compared with the classical Lagrange multiplier (LM) approach, the RLM schemes ensure that the resulting discrete system is uniquely solvable over a broad range of time steps. The key idea is to augment the LM formulation with a relaxation term, yielding a scalar quadratic equation for the multiplier with an explicit closed-form solution. The resulting schemes are linear and efficient because each time step requires solving only two linear systems with constant coefficients, at a cost comparable to that of SAV schemes. We construct both first-order and second-order variants and prove their energy stability. Numerical experiments verify the expected convergence rates and demonstrate that the RLM schemes accurately capture interface dynamics.

    Abstract

    We propose a new class of second-order, linear, unconditionally energy-stable schemes for the Cahn--Hilliard--Navier--Stokes (CHNS) system. A single scalar Lagrange multiplier is introduced to enforce the discrete energy dissipation law for the coupled system. At each time step, the phase-field variables, chemical potential, and velocity are obtained by solving four independent linear equations, after which the multiplier is updated via a single scalar quadratic equation with an explicit closed-form solution. This contrasts with other existing numerical schemes for the CHNS system. We provide rigorous proofs that unconditional energy stability holds for the proposed schemes. Numerical experiments are then presented to confirm second-order temporal accuracy. Compared with other existing numerical schemes for the CHNS system, our schemes achieve better or comparable accuracy and efficiency.

  • Q. Hong, J. Zhao and Y. Gong*Linearized supplementary variable method (LSVM) for thermodynamically consistent partial differential equationsComputer Methods in Applied Mechanics and Engineering, 461, 119251, 2026.DOI
    Abstract

    [1] Thermodynamically consistent partial differential equations (TCPDEs) arise broadly in science and engineering. Preserving their energy dissipation structure at the discrete level is important for robustness, but is computationally challenging due to the stiff nonlinearities in TCPDEs. The difficulty grows when the mobility operator is state-dependent. In this setting, reformulation-based approaches such as IEQ and SAV typically yield variable-coefficient linear systems and lose their main computational advantage. We propose a linearized supplementary variable method (LSVM) for TCPDEs with a broad class of state-dependent mobilities that admit a splitting into a constant-coefficient core and a state-dependent remainder. The method introduces a balancing mechanism that enforces the energy law. The resulting second-order Crank--Nicolson and BDF2 schemes are constant-coefficient and linear, requiring only the solution of a small number of linear problems with a fixed, prefactorable operator, plus an additional 2 \ttimes 2 linear system per time step. We prove energy-law preservation and unique solvability. Numerical experiments on the Gross--Pitaevskii equation, the molecular beam epitaxy model with slope selection, and the Cahn--Hilliard equation with variable mobility show that the proposed schemes permit significantly larger time steps than many existing implicit methods, while maintaining comparable accuracy. We observe substantial efficiency gains in all test problems.

  • D. Yang, J. Zhao, X. Zhang and M. Jiang*Operator splitting decoupled energy-stable numerical schemes for phase field grain growth modelsJournal of Computational Physics, 544, 114405, 2026.DOI
    Abstract

    Phase-field methods have been widely employed to study grain growth in materials science. These grain-growth phase-field models are typically derived from variational principles, which ensure thermodynamic consistency and energy dissipation properties. Numerical discretizations that inherit these properties are known as energy-stable or structure-preserving schemes. However, existing energy-stable schemes for phase-field grain-growth models typically require solving fully coupled systems, which are computationally expensive, especially when the number of grains is large. In this paper, we develop a class of computationally efficient, fully decoupled numerical schemes based on operator splitting techniques. We present this decoupling idea by integrating it with several existing energy-stable strategies, including Energy Quadratization (EQ), Relaxed Energy Quadratization (REQ), the stabilization method, and the convex splitting method. The resulting schemes are unconditionally energy-stable, i.e., they satisfy a discrete energy dissipation law without restrictions on the time-step sizes. The proposed schemes are efficient because they only require updating each grain through small, independent subproblems at each time step. We present first and second-order formulations and provide rigorous energy analyses. Numerical convergence tests and benchmark examples are presented to validate the accuracy, efficiency, and robustness of our proposed decoupled numerical methods for simulating large grain systems.

  • Q. Hong, Z. Zhang♠ and J. Zhao*Auxiliary relaxation method to derive thermodynamically consistent phase field models with constraints and structure preserving numerical approximationsJournal of Computational Physics, 522, 113598, 2025.DOI
    Abstract

    In this paper, we introduce a novel approach for formulating phase field models with constraints. The main idea is to introduce auxiliary variables that regularize and gradually dissipate constraint deviations of the phase variables, which we name the auxiliary relaxation method. It integrates seamlessly with the energy variational framework to ensure thermodynamic consistency in the resulting phase field models. Unlike traditional penalty methods, which introduce high stiffness due to large penalty parameters to enforce constraints in phase field models, our approach reduces system stiffness, allowing larger time step sizes when solving phase field models with constraints numerically, thus improving numerical accuracy and efficiency. We demonstrate the effectiveness and robustness of the proposed auxiliary relaxation method by applying it across several scenarios to derive thermodynamically consistent phase field models with constraints. Furthermore, we introduce a general second-order implicit-explicit Crank-Nicolson scheme, combining the relaxed scalar auxiliary variable method with a stabilization technique to solve these models. Through extensive numerical tests, we validate the capability of our modeling and numerical framework to reliably simulate complex dynamics governed by phase field equations with constraints.

  • Z. Zhang♠ and J. Zhao*General numerical framework to derive structure preserving reduced order models for thermodynamically consistent reversible-irreversible PDEsJournal of Computational Physics, 521, 113562, 2025.DOI
    Abstract

    In this paper, we propose a general numerical framework to derive structure-preserving reduced-order models for thermodynamically consistent PDEs. Our numerical framework has two primary features: (a) a systematic way to extract reduced-order models for thermodynamically consistent PDE systems while maintaining their inherent thermodynamic principles and (b) a general process to derive accurate, efficient, and structure-preserving numerical algorithms to solve these reduced-order models. The platform's generality extends to various PDE systems governed by embedded thermodynamic laws, offering a unique approach from several perspectives. First, it utilizes the generalized Onsager principle to transform the thermodynamically consistent PDE system into an equivalent form, where the free energy of the transformed system takes a quadratic form in terms of the state variables. This transformation is known as energy quadratization (EQ). Through EQ, we gain a novel perspective on deriving reduced-order models that continue to respect the energy dissipation law. Secondly, our proposed numerical approach automatically provides algorithms to discretize these reduced-order models. The proposed algorithms are always linear, easy to implement and solve, and uniquely solvable. Furthermore, these algorithms inherently ensure the thermodynamic laws. Our platform offers a distinctive approach for deriving structure-preserving reduced-order models for a wide range of PDE systems with underlying thermodynamic principles.

  • J. Zhang, X. Guo, M. Jiang, T. Zhou and J. Zhao*Linear relaxation method with regularized energy reformulation for phase field modelsJournal of Computational Physics, 515, 113225, 2024.DOI
    Abstract

    In this paper, we establish a novel linear relaxation method with regularized energy reformulation for phase field models, which we name the RRER method. We employ the molecular beam epitaxy (MBE) model and the phase-field crystal (PFC) model as test beds, along with several coupled phase field models, to illustrate the concept. Our proposed RRER strategy is applicable to a broad class of phase field models that can be derived through energy variation. The RRER method consists of two major steps. First, we introduce regularized auxiliary variables to reformulate the original phase field models into equivalent forms where the free energy is transformed under the auxiliary variables. Then, we discretize the reformulated PDE model under these variables on a staggered time mesh for the phase field models. We incorporate the energy reformulation idea in the first step and the linear relaxation concept in the second step to derive a general numerical algorithm for phase field models that is linear and second-order accurate. Our approach differs from the classical invariant energy quadratization (IEQ) and scalar auxiliary variable (SAV) approaches, as we don't need to take time derivatives for the auxiliary variables. The resulting schemes are linear, i.e., only linear algebraic systems need to be solved at each time step. Rigorous theoretical analysis demonstrates that these resulting schemes satisfy the modified discrete energy dissipation laws and preserve the discrete mass conservation for the PFC and MBE models. Furthermore, we present numerical results to demonstrate the effectiveness of our method in solving phase field models.
